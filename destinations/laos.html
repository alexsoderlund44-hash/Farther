Laos on a budget

Getting around

Minivans and buses link the main towns, the two-day slow boat runs down the Mekong from Huay Xai, and the Laos-China Railway connects Vientiane, Vang Vieng and Luang Prabang quickly. Train tickets often sell out, so buy them a few days ahead. Tuk-tuks handle short trips, and the LOCA app works in Vientiane.

Where to sleep

Family guesthouses are the standard cheap option, with hostel dorms in Luang Prabang and Vang Vieng and village homestays around Nong Khiaw.

What to eat

  • Laap: minced meat salad with herbs, lime and toasted rice
  • Khao piak sen: thick rice noodle soup, a common breakfast
  • Tam mak hoong: spicy green papaya salad
  • Beerlao: the light national lager, sold everywhere

Money

Carry cash kip, since ATMs have low withdrawal limits and fees, and cards work only at some hotels and tour offices. Tipping isn't expected, and gentle haggling is fine at markets and with tuk-tuk drivers.

Phone data

SIMs from Unitel or Lao Telecom are cheap and need your passport, with decent coverage in towns but gaps in the mountains and along the river.

Heads up

Unexploded ordnance from the Vietnam War remains in rural areas, so stay on marked paths when hiking.

Laos travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Laos cost on a budget?

About $29 a day for one person, from $20 in Muang Ngoi Neua to $31 in Kong Lor Cave. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $203 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Laos?

November to March is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Laos?

Muang Ngoi Neua, at about $20 a day. Reachable only by river boat, this sleepy village offers caves, swimming holes and easy walks to Hmong villages beneath towering karst cliffs.
