Getting around

Buses are the main way around and cover most routes, while the Sarajevo to Mostar train is short, scenic and cheap. Big ride-hailing apps are not widely used, so use metered taxis or ask your host to call one. Bus seats are rarely sold out, but buying the day before helps in summer.

Where to sleep

Hostels and family-run guesthouses are cheap in Sarajevo and Mostar, and village homestays are the way to stay in places like Lukomir. Mostar gets busy with day-trippers in summer, but rooms are usually easy to find.

What to eat

  • Ćevapi: small grilled sausages in flatbread with raw onion
  • Burek: spiral pie filled with minced meat
  • Bosnian coffee: strong coffee served in a small copper pot
  • Tufahija: poached apple stuffed with walnuts and topped with cream

Money

Bring cash in convertible marks for buses, small cafes and guesthouses, though cards work in cities. Rounding up the bill or leaving about 10 percent is normal in restaurants.

Phone data

A BH Telecom or m:tel SIM is cheap and easy to buy at kiosks with your passport, and coverage is good in towns, though patchy in mountains.

Heads up

Landmines remain in some rural areas, so stay on marked paths and paved roads when hiking.

Bosnia and Herzegovina travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Bosnia and Herzegovina cost on a budget?

About $33 a day for one person, from $30 in Travnik to $42 in Lukomir. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $231 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Bosnia and Herzegovina?

May to September is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Bosnia and Herzegovina?

Travnik, at about $30 a day. Former seat of the Ottoman viziers, with a hilltop fortress, the painted Šarena mosque, the Plava Voda springs and its well-known Travnik cheese.

How long do you need in Bosnia and Herzegovina?

Sarajevo, the train to Mostar, with Blagaj, Kravica and the mountain village of Lukomir. 10 days.

How much is $1 in Bosnia and Herzegovina?

One US dollar is about 1.74 Convertible mark (BAM), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 34.77 BAM, and a typical budget day of $33 comes to about 57.00 BAM.

How do you get around Bosnia and Herzegovina cheaply?

Buses are the main way around and cover most routes, while the Sarajevo to Mostar train is short, scenic and cheap. Big ride-hailing apps are not widely used, so use metered taxis or ask your host to call one. Bus seats are rarely sold out, but buying the day before helps in summer.

What food should you try in Bosnia and Herzegovina?

Ćevapi, small grilled sausages in flatbread with raw onion. Burek, spiral pie filled with minced meat. Bosnian coffee, strong coffee served in a small copper pot. Tufahija, poached apple stuffed with walnuts and topped with cream.
