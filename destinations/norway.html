Getting around

Vy trains run the scenic Bergen line, and cheap fares appear when you book early on the Vy or Entur apps. Ferries and express boats are part of the public network in the fjords, and buses serve the Lofoten Islands.

Where to sleep

Hostels, campsites with cabins, and Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT) huts keep costs down. Lofoten rorbuer and fjord campsites book up in summer, and the right to roam allows free wild camping.

What to eat

  • Brunost: sweet brown caramel-like cheese, sliced onto bread
  • Fiskesuppe: creamy fish soup, a Bergen classic
  • Pølse i lompe: hot dog wrapped in a soft potato flatbread
  • Skillingsboller: large cinnamon buns from Bergen bakeries

Money

Cards work everywhere, even at tiny kiosks, and cash is rarely needed. Tipping is not expected, though rounding up for good service is welcome.

Phone data

Prepaid SIMs from Telia, Telenor or Ice are easy to buy, EU SIMs roam free here as Norway is in the EEA, and coverage is strong even in many fjords.

Heads up

Mountain weather changes quickly, so check the forecast and the hiking advice before longer hikes, even in summer.

Norway travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Norway cost on a budget?

About $94 a day for one person, from $84 in Odda to $150 in Svalbard. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $658 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Norway?

June to August is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Norway?

Odda, at about $84 a day. An industrial fjord town that serves as the base for the long hike to the cliff-edge rock of Trolltunga.

How long do you need in Norway?

Oslo, Bergen and the fjords, then the Lofoten Islands. 2 weeks.

One US dollar is about 9.62 Norwegian krone (NOK), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 192 NOK, and a typical budget day of $94 comes to about 900 NOK.
