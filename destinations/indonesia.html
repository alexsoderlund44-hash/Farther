Getting around

On Java, KAI trains are cheap and comfortable, buses and ferries link the islands, and domestic flights save days on long hops. Grab and Gojek run in most cities and on Bali for motorbike rides, cars and food delivery. Book trains ahead around Lebaran (the end of Ramadan), when the whole country travels.

Where to sleep

Family homestays and guesthouses are the best value, with hostel dorms on Bali and in Yogyakarta and simple homestays on Flores.

What to eat

  • Nasi goreng: fried rice, often topped with a fried egg
  • Gado-gado: vegetables with peanut sauce, tofu and egg
  • Rendang: beef slow-cooked in coconut milk and spices
  • Sate: grilled meat skewers with peanut sauce

Money

Cash is king outside the cities, and ATMs are common but often have low withdrawal limits; use bank ATMs over street money changers. Tipping is optional, and haggling is normal at markets and with drivers.

Phone data

A Telkomsel SIM gives the widest coverage and is sold at airports with passport registration, though an eSIM is simpler for short stays.

Heads up

On Bali, some small money changers short-change you with sleight of hand, so count your cash carefully or use an ATM.

Indonesia travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Indonesia cost on a budget?

About $37 a day for one person, from $22 in Lake Toba to $72 in Mount Rinjani. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $259 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Indonesia?

May to September is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Indonesia?

Lake Toba, at about $22 a day. A vast crater lake in Sumatra, with traditional Batak villages, carved tombs and swimming spots on Samosir Island.

How long do you need in Indonesia?

Yogyakarta, overland to Bali, then east to Flores. 3 to 4 weeks.

One US dollar is about 17,915 Indonesian rupiah (IDR), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 358,302 IDR, and a typical budget day of $37 comes to about 660,000 IDR.

On Java, KAI trains are cheap and comfortable, buses and ferries link the islands, and domestic flights save days on long hops. Grab and Gojek run in most cities and on Bali for motorbike rides, cars and food delivery. Book trains ahead around Lebaran (the end of Ramadan), when the whole country travels.
