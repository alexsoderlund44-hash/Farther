Mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. Cash machines and exchange desks pay a little less.

Getting around

Long-distance buses with semi-cama and cama seats cover the country, but distances are huge, so budget flights on Aerolíneas Argentinas, Flybondi or JetSMART often make sense for Patagonia. Uber, Cabify and DiDi work in Buenos Aires and other big cities. Book flights and El Chaltén beds early for the December to February peak.

Where to sleep

Hostel dorms are common in Buenos Aires, Mendoza and El Chaltén, and Patagonia has campsites, including free ones near El Chaltén's trails.

What to eat

  • Asado: slow-grilled beef and sausages cooked over wood or charcoal
  • Empanadas: baked pastries with regional fillings like beef or cheese
  • Choripán: grilled chorizo sausage in bread with chimichurri
  • Mate: bitter herbal tea shared from a gourd with a metal straw

Money

Foreign cards usually get a fair exchange rate now, while local ATMs charge high fees and have low withdrawal limits, so many travelers pay by card and keep some cash for small purchases. Tipping about 10% in restaurants is normal.

Phone data

Personal, Claro and Movistar SIMs are sold with a passport, and coverage is good in cities but very limited on Patagonian roads and trails.

Heads up

If you're offered mate, it's polite to drink the whole gourd before passing it back, and saying "gracias" means you're done.

Argentina travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Argentina cost on a budget?

About $73 a day for one person, from $38 in Iruya to $87 in El Calafate. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $511 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Argentina?

November to March is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

Iruya, at about $38 a day. Village clinging to a mountainside at the end of a dramatic dirt road, with walks to the even more remote hamlet of San Isidro.
