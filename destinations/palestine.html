Getting around

Arab buses from near Damascus Gate in East Jerusalem run to Bethlehem, and shared yellow minibuses (servees) link West Bank towns. Taxis are cheap, but agree the fare first. Checkpoints can add delays, so allow extra time and carry your passport.

Where to sleep

Family guesthouses and small hotels in Bethlehem are the usual budget base, and homestays can be arranged in some villages.

What to eat

  • Musakhan: roast chicken with sumac and onions on taboon bread
  • Maqluba: upside-down rice with vegetables and chicken
  • Knafeh Nabulsiyeh: Nablus-style sweet cheese pastry in syrup
  • Manakish: flatbread topped with za'atar and olive oil

Money

Israeli shekels are the main currency, and Jordanian dinars are also used for some larger payments; cash is king, though ATMs are available in Bethlehem and Ramallah. Haggle in markets, and round up or tip about 10% in restaurants.

Phone data

Local SIMs from Jawwal or Ooredoo are sold with your passport, and Israeli SIMs and eSIMs also work in most of the West Bank; coverage is good in towns.

Heads up

Dress modestly in towns like Hebron, and don't photograph soldiers or checkpoints.
