Getting around

Buses and furgons (minibuses) are the main way around, leaving from street-side stops or bus terminals with schedules that are often only known locally. There are no big ride-hailing apps, so agree a taxi fare before you get in. For Theth, book a seat on a minibus from Shkodër a day ahead, and if you plan the Valbona pass, arrange the Komani Lake ferry back through your guesthouse.

Where to sleep

Guesthouses and small family-run hotels are cheap and common, and Tirana, Berat and Gjirokastër have good hostels. Theth guesthouses usually include dinner and breakfast, and the Riviera fills fast in July and August.

What to eat

  • Byrek: flaky filo pie with cheese, spinach or meat
  • Tavë kosi: baked lamb and rice in a tangy yogurt custard
  • Fërgesë: peppers, tomatoes and cottage cheese baked into a rich dip
  • Qofte: grilled spiced meat patties, usually served with bread

Money

Cash rules outside Tirana and the bigger hotels, and many ATMs charge a fixed fee, so take out larger amounts at once. Rounding up or leaving about 10 percent in restaurants is appreciated.

Phone data

A local prepaid SIM is cheap and easy to buy in operator shops with your passport, with good coverage in towns but weak signal in the northern mountains.

Heads up

A nod often means no and a head shake means yes, so confirm answers in words.

Albania travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Albania cost on a budget?

About $36 a day for one person, from $31 in Korçë to $56 in Lake Koman. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $252 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Albania?

May to June and September to October is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

Korçë, at about $31 a day. An old bazaar, a local brewery and cobbled streets give this southeastern town a relaxed charm, with Voskopojë's frescoed churches nearby.
