Namibia on a budget

Red dunes at Sossusvlei, rhinos at Etosha's waterholes and long empty roads. Namibia is best seen on a budget by splitting a rental car and camping under huge desert skies.

Getting around

Public transport is limited, so most visitors rent a car, often a 4x4 with a rooftop tent. Intercape and shuttle minibuses link Windhoek with larger towns like Swakopmund. Book rental cars and park campsites months ahead for the July to October season.

Where to sleep

Camping is the main budget option, and campsites are well run with water and braai areas. Windhoek and Swakopmund have hostels, and camps near Sossusvlei and in Etosha fill early.

What to eat

  • Kapana: grilled street beef strips, eaten with spicy salt
  • Oshifima: stiff millet porridge from the north
  • Potjiekos: slow-cooked stew made in a three-legged pot
  • Game meat: oryx or kudu steak, common on menus

Money

Cards work in towns and most lodges, and ATMs are common in towns. The South African rand is accepted at par, though Namibian dollars aren't accepted in South Africa, and about 10 percent is a normal tip.

Phone data

MTC SIMs have the widest coverage and need your passport, but large stretches of desert have no signal at all.

Heads up

Gravel roads cause many rollovers, so drive slowly and avoid driving after dark.

Namibia travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Namibia cost on a budget?

About $70 a day for one person, from $38 in Windhoek to $72 in Skeleton Coast. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $490 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Namibia?

May to October is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Namibia?

Windhoek, at about $38 a day. A small, orderly capital with German colonial buildings and a lively craft market, the starting point for most Namibian road trips.

How long do you need in Namibia?

Windhoek, Sossusvlei, Swakopmund and Etosha. 2 weeks.

How much is $1 in Namibia?

One US dollar is about 16.65 Namibian dollar (NAD), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 333 NAD, and a typical budget day of $70 comes to about 1,200 NAD.

How do you get around Namibia cheaply?

Public transport is limited, so most visitors rent a car, often a 4x4 with a rooftop tent. Intercape and shuttle minibuses link Windhoek with larger towns like Swakopmund. Book rental cars and park campsites months ahead for the July to October season.

What food should you try in Namibia?

Kapana, grilled street beef strips, eaten with spicy salt. Oshifima, stiff millet porridge from the north. Potjiekos, slow-cooked stew made in a three-legged pot. Game meat, oryx or kudu steak, common on menus.
