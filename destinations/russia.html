Getting around

Russian Railways (RZD) trains run between St Petersburg and Moscow and across Siberia, with cheap open-plan platskart berths on long routes. Yandex Go is the main ride-hailing app, but it may not accept foreign-issued cards.

Where to sleep

Hostels are common in Moscow and St Petersburg and along the Trans-Siberian route, and foreigners must be registered by their accommodation.

What to eat

  • Pelmeni: small meat dumplings with sour cream
  • Blini: thin pancakes with sweet or savory fillings
  • Shchi: cabbage soup, a staple of Russian home cooking
  • Kvass: lightly fermented drink made from rye bread

Money

Western bank cards do not work in Russia, so visitors have to bring enough cash, usually euros or dollars, to exchange. Tipping about 10 percent in restaurants is common.

Phone data

Buying a local SIM requires a passport and registration, many foreign eSIMs do not work there, and mobile internet can be restricted or shut down in some areas.

Heads up

Foreigners have been detained on vague charges, so avoid photographing military or government sites and avoid political conversations or posts.

Russia travel: quick answers

About $42 a day for one person, from $35 in Kazan to $57 in Kizhi Island. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $294 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Russia?

June to September is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.
