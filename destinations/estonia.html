Getting around

Elron trains and frequent intercity buses link Tallinn with Tartu and the coast, while Lahemaa is easier with a car or tour. Ferries to Saaremaa are quick but busy in summer, so book car spaces ahead; Bolt started here and works everywhere.

Where to sleep

Tallinn has many hostels, and rural Estonia has guesthouses and free forest campsites run by the state forest agency (RMK).

What to eat

  • Kiluvõileib: rye bread with spiced sprats, egg and butter
  • Rukkileib: dark rye bread served with almost every meal
  • Kama: roasted grain flour mixed with kefir or buttermilk
  • Verivorst: blood sausage, traditionally eaten at Christmas

Money

Cards and contactless payments are accepted almost everywhere, even for small amounts. Tipping is not expected, but rounding up or 10 percent for good service is welcome.

Phone data

Prepaid SIMs from Telia, Elisa or Tele2 are easy to buy, EU SIMs roam free, and coverage is excellent, with free Wi-Fi in many public places.

Heads up

Pedestrians must wear a reflector in the dark outside lit areas, and police can fine you for not having one.

Estonia travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Estonia cost on a budget?

About $53 a day for one person, from $44 in Tartu to $54 in Soomaa. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $371 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Estonia?

May to September is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Estonia?

Tartu, at about $44 a day. Estonia's university town mixes cosy student bars and riverside parks with the striking Estonian National Museum.

How long do you need in Estonia?

Tallinn, Lahemaa and Saaremaa. 5 days.

How much is $1 in Estonia?

One US dollar is about 0.889 Euro (EUR), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 17.78 EUR, and a typical budget day of $53 comes to about 47.00 EUR.

How do you get around Estonia cheaply?

Elron trains and frequent intercity buses link Tallinn with Tartu and the coast, while Lahemaa is easier with a car or tour. Ferries to Saaremaa are quick but busy in summer, so book car spaces ahead; Bolt started here and works everywhere.

What food should you try in Estonia?

Kiluvõileib, rye bread with spiced sprats, egg and butter. Rukkileib, dark rye bread served with almost every meal. Kama, roasted grain flour mixed with kefir or buttermilk. Verivorst, blood sausage, traditionally eaten at Christmas.
