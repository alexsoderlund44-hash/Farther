Getting around

In Abidjan, Yango and Heetch work for rides, and woro-woro shared taxis, gbaka minibuses and lagoon water buses are very cheap. Comfortable intercity bus companies run to Yamoussoukro and Grand-Bassam, with shared minibuses to Assinie. Buses fill fast on holiday weekends, so buy tickets at the depot a day ahead.

Where to sleep

Budget stays are simple hotels, residences and short-let apartments, with only a few hostels in Abidjan. Grand-Bassam and Assinie have beach bungalows that get busy on weekends, so book ahead for Friday and Saturday nights.

What to eat

  • Attiéké poisson: fermented cassava couscous with grilled fish
  • Alloco: fried ripe plantain with chili and onion
  • Garba: attiéké with fried tuna, a cheap street staple
  • Kedjenou: chicken slow-cooked with vegetables in a sealed pot

Money

Cash is still king, though ATMs are common in Abidjan and mobile money is everywhere. Bargain at markets and with unmetered taxis; rounding up or a small tip is appreciated in restaurants.

Phone data

Orange, MTN and Moov SIMs are cheap and require passport registration, and 4G coverage is strong in the south and main cities.

Heads up

The Atlantic surf at Grand-Bassam and Assinie has dangerous rip currents, so swim only where locals do and in calm conditions.

How much does a day in Côte d'Ivoire cost on a budget?

About $42 a day for one person, from $30 in Man to $85 in Taï National Park. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $294 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Côte d'Ivoire?

November to March is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Côte d'Ivoire?

Man, at about $30 a day. A green mountain town ringed by forests and waterfalls, below the tooth-shaped Dent de Man peak.

Abidjan, Grand-Bassam, Assinie and Yamoussoukro. 10 days.
