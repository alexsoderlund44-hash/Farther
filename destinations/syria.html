Getting around

Most visitors travel with a specialist operator who arranges the driver, permits and route. Intercity buses connect Damascus, Homs and Aleppo, and taxis are cheap in the cities. Don't plan independent travel to areas outside government-held cities without current local advice.

Where to sleep

Small hotels and a few converted courtyard houses in the old cities of Damascus and Aleppo are the usual options, often booked through your operator.

What to eat

  • Shawarma: spit-roasted chicken or meat wrapped in thin bread
  • Fatteh: chickpeas, yogurt and toasted bread with tahini
  • Kebab karaz: Aleppo kebab cooked in sour cherry sauce
  • Muhammara: roasted red pepper and walnut dip

Money

Foreign bank cards generally don't work, so bring all your money in clean US dollar or euro notes and change it at official exchange offices. Small tips for drivers and guides are customary, and haggling is normal in souks.

Phone data

Local SIMs from Syriatel or MTN Syria are sold with a passport, often with your operator's help; coverage is fair in cities, and connections can be slow.

Heads up

Never photograph checkpoints, soldiers or government buildings, and follow your guide's advice on where to go.

Syria travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Syria cost on a budget?

About $35 a day for one person, from $26 in Hama to $41 in Krak des Chevaliers. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $245 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Syria?

April to May and October to November is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

Hama, at about $26 a day. Giant wooden waterwheels, the norias, creak and turn slowly on the Orontes River in this historic city.
