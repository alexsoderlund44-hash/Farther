Getting around

Buses and trains are frequent and cheap, and you pay with a Rav-Kav card or a payment app; Moovit is the best app for routes. Most public transport stops from Friday afternoon to Saturday evening, though shared sherut minibuses run on some routes. Gett and Yango are the usual taxi apps.

Where to sleep

Hostels with dorms are found in Tel Aviv, Jerusalem, Haifa and the Galilee, and some offer rooftop beds. Book ahead for Jewish holidays, when prices rise and beds sell out.

What to eat

  • Sabich: pita with fried eggplant, boiled egg and tahini
  • Shakshuka: eggs poached in spicy tomato and pepper sauce
  • Hummus: chickpea and tahini dip, eaten as a full meal
  • Falafel: fried chickpea balls stuffed into pita with salads

Money

Cards are accepted almost everywhere, and ATMs are easy to find. Tipping 10 to 15% is expected in restaurants, and haggling is normal in markets like Jerusalem's Old City.

Phone data

An eSIM is the simplest choice, though prepaid tourist SIMs are also sold at the airport and in phone shops; coverage is good across the country.

Heads up

Learn where the nearest shelter is wherever you stay, and install the official Home Front Command alert app.

Israel travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Israel cost on a budget?

About $84 a day for one person, from $62 in Nazareth to $85 in Masada. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $588 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Israel?

March to May and October to November is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Israel?

Nazareth, at about $62 a day. Arab city around the Basilica of the Annunciation, with a lively old market, Ottoman mansions and some of the country's best knafeh.

Tel Aviv, Jerusalem, the Dead Sea and Masada, then north to the Galilee. 10 days.
