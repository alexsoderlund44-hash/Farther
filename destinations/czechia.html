Getting around

Trains and buses are cheap, with RegioJet, Leo Express and the national ČD competing on main routes. Buses are often the better option for Český Krumlov, and Bolt and Uber work in Prague and Brno.

Where to sleep

Prague has plenty of hostel dorms, and small towns have cheap family pensions (penzion). Český Krumlov fills up on summer weekends, so book ahead.

What to eat

  • Svíčková: beef in creamy vegetable sauce with bread dumplings
  • Smažený sýr: fried breaded cheese with tartar sauce
  • Bramboráky: crisp garlicky potato pancakes
  • Kofola: Czech herbal cola, often on tap

Money

The currency is the koruna, and cards are widely accepted. Always pay in koruna rather than accepting a conversion to your home currency, and leave about 10 percent in restaurants.

Phone data

Prepaid SIMs from O2, T-Mobile or Vodafone are easy to buy, EU SIMs roam free, and coverage is good across the country.

Get an eSIM for Czechia

Heads up

Avoid street money changers and exchange booths with 'zero commission' signs, and order taxis through an app instead of hailing them in central Prague.

Czechia travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Czechia cost on a budget?

About $51 a day for one person, from $41 in Olomouc to $52 in Moravian Karst. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $357 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Czechia?

May to September is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Czechia?

Olomouc, at about $41 a day. Baroque fountains and a towering Holy Trinity column fill the squares of this university city, with far fewer tourists than Prague.

How long do you need in Czechia?

Prague, Český Krumlov and Bohemian Switzerland. 1 week.

How much is $1 in Czechia?

One US dollar is about 21.73 Czech koruna (CZK), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 435 CZK, and a typical budget day of $51 comes to about 1,100 CZK.

How do you get around Czechia cheaply?

Trains and buses are cheap, with RegioJet, Leo Express and the national ČD competing on main routes. Buses are often the better option for Český Krumlov, and Bolt and Uber work in Prague and Brno.
