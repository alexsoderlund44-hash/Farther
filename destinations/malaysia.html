Getting around

Long-distance buses are cheap and comfortable, ETS trains run along the west coast, and AirAsia flies to Borneo and Langkawi. Grab is widely used in cities. Ferries to the Perhentians run from Kuala Besut, and many island businesses close during the northeast monsoon from roughly November to February.

Where to sleep

Hostels and guesthouses are cheap in Kuala Lumpur, Penang and the Cameron Highlands, and island rooms should be booked ahead in peak season.

What to eat

  • Nasi lemak: coconut rice with sambal, anchovies, peanuts and egg
  • Char kway teow: smoky fried flat rice noodles with prawns
  • Roti canai: flaky flatbread with curry for dipping
  • Teh tarik: strong milky tea poured until frothy

Money

Cards work in cities, but hawker stalls and small shops prefer cash, and ATMs are common. Tipping isn't expected, and haggling is fine at night markets.

Phone data

Prepaid SIMs from CelcomDigi or Hotlink are sold at airports and need a passport, and coverage is good in towns.

Malaysia travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Malaysia cost on a budget?

About $26 a day for one person, from $21 in Ipoh to $98 in Mount Kinabalu. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $182 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Malaysia?

March to September is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Malaysia?

Ipoh, at about $21 a day. Buddhist cave temples carved into limestone, colonial streets with murals and creamy white coffee make Ipoh a great food stop.

How long do you need in Malaysia?

Kuala Lumpur, the Cameron Highlands, Penang, then across to the Perhentians. 2 to 3 weeks.

How much is $1 in Malaysia?

One US dollar is about 4.08 Malaysian ringgit (MYR), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 81.69 MYR, and a typical budget day of $26 comes to about 110 MYR.

How do you get around Malaysia cheaply?

Long-distance buses are cheap and comfortable, ETS trains run along the west coast, and AirAsia flies to Borneo and Langkawi. Grab is widely used in cities. Ferries to the Perhentians run from Kuala Besut, and many island businesses close during the northeast monsoon from roughly November to February.

What food should you try in Malaysia?

Nasi lemak, coconut rice with sambal, anchovies, peanuts and egg. Char kway teow, smoky fried flat rice noodles with prawns. Roti canai, flaky flatbread with curry for dipping. Teh tarik, strong milky tea poured until frothy.
