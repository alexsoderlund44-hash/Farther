Cameroon on a budget

'Africa in miniature', with a volcano, black-sand beaches and rainforest. Parts of the country carry travel warnings, so stick to areas like Limbe and Kribi and check current advice.

Getting around

Agency buses and minibuses run between Douala, Limbe and Kribi, and the Camrail train links Douala with Yaoundé. In towns, shared yellow taxis and moto-taxis are cheap; state your destination and price before getting in. Limbe and Mount Cameroon are in the Southwest region, which is covered by the advisory, so check current advice before planning that leg.

Where to sleep

Budget beds are simple hotels, auberges and mission guesthouses, with very few hostels. Mount Cameroon treks use basic huts or camping arranged through local guides.

What to eat

  • Ndolé: bitterleaf and peanut stew, often with shrimp or beef
  • Poulet DG: chicken fried with plantains and vegetables
  • Soya: spicy grilled beef skewers sold at night
  • Puff-puff: fried sweet dough balls, a common snack

Money

Cameroon is mostly cash, and ATMs in Douala and Yaoundé generally take Visa, with fewer options in small towns. Haggle at markets; tipping is modest and not expected at street food stalls.

Phone data

MTN and Orange SIMs are cheap and need passport registration, and coverage is decent in cities but patchy in rural and mountain areas.

Heads up

Checkpoints are common on intercity roads, so always carry your passport and keep things calm and polite.

About $32 a day for one person, from $30 in Foumban to $47 in Mount Cameroon. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $224 before flights.

November to March is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.
