Getting around

The Haramain high-speed train links Jeddah and Medina, other trains connect Riyadh with the north and east, and SAPTCO buses run between cities. Careem and Uber operate in the major cities, and cheap domestic flights on flynas and flyadeal are useful for long distances. AlUla is best reached by flight, and you'll need a car or tour once there.

Where to sleep

Hostels are still rare, so budget hotels and apartment rentals are the usual cheap beds. In AlUla and the desert, camps offer tents at a range of prices, and they fill up in the cooler winter season.

What to eat

  • Kabsa: spiced rice with chicken or lamb, the national dish
  • Mutabbaq: pan-fried stuffed pastry, a street food favorite
  • Saudi coffee: light cardamom coffee served with dates
  • Jareesh: cracked wheat cooked slowly with meat and spices

Money

Cards and phone payments are accepted almost everywhere, even for small purchases. Tipping isn't required but rounding up is appreciated, and haggling is fine in traditional souks.

Phone data

Prepaid SIMs from STC, Mobily or Zain need your passport and a quick registration, often at the airport, and coverage is good in cities and along highways.

Heads up

Non-Muslims cannot enter Mecca or the central area of Medina, and during Ramadan you shouldn't eat or drink in public in daylight.

How much does a day in Saudi Arabia cost on a budget?

About $90 a day for one person, from $58 in Taif to $105 in Hegra. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $630 before flights.

November to March is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.
