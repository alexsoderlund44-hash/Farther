Getting around

Most visitors travel with a specialist operator who handles transport, since road travel is risky and checkpoints are frequent. Domestic flights link Kabul, Herat and Mazar-i-Sharif, and there are no ride-hailing apps.

Where to sleep

Hotels are usually arranged by your tour operator, and options in Bamiyan are simple guesthouses.

What to eat

  • Kabuli pulao: rice with lamb, carrots and raisins
  • Mantu: steamed dumplings topped with yogurt and lentils
  • Bolani: thin flatbread stuffed with potato or leeks
  • Green tea: served at every meal and meeting

Money

Afghanistan is cash only, foreign cards don't work, and you will need to bring US dollars to exchange. Tipping drivers and guides is customary.

Phone data

Roshan and AWCC SIMs need a passport, but mobile internet can be slow or cut off.

Heads up

Never photograph people, especially women, or checkpoints without permission.

Afghanistan travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Afghanistan cost on a budget?

About $46 a day for one person, from $38 in Mazar-i-Sharif to $48 in Kabul. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $322 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Afghanistan?

April to June and September to October is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Afghanistan?

Mazar-i-Sharif, at about $38 a day. Northern city centred on the blue-tiled Shrine of Hazrat Ali, famous for its white pigeons, with ancient Balkh ruins nearby.

How long do you need in Afghanistan?

Kabul, Bamiyan and Band-e-Amir, then Herat. 10 days, only with a specialist operator.

How much is $1 in Afghanistan?

One US dollar is about 64.84 Afghan afghani (AFN), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 1,297 AFN, and a typical budget day of $46 comes to about 3,000 AFN.

How do you get around Afghanistan cheaply?

Most visitors travel with a specialist operator who handles transport, since road travel is risky and checkpoints are frequent. Domestic flights link Kabul, Herat and Mazar-i-Sharif, and there are no ride-hailing apps.

What food should you try in Afghanistan?

Kabuli pulao, rice with lamb, carrots and raisins. Mantu, steamed dumplings topped with yogurt and lentils. Bolani, thin flatbread stuffed with potato or leeks. Green tea, served at every meal and meeting.

Is it safe to travel to Afghanistan?

Afghanistan is governed by the Taliban, and most governments advise against all travel because of terrorism, kidnapping and arbitrary detention. Many insurers won't cover you, and consular help is very limited. Visitors usually travel only with a specialist tour operator. Check your government's current travel advice before planning a trip.
