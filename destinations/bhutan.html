Mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. Cash machines and exchange desks pay a little less.

Getting around

Most visitors get around by hired car with a driver, arranged before arrival. Public buses link the main towns but are slow and infrequent, and there are no ride-hailing apps.

Where to sleep

Tourist-certified hotels are the main option, and farmstays offer a homely, good-value alternative.

What to eat

  • Ema datshi: chilies cooked in cheese, the national dish
  • Momos: steamed dumplings with meat, cheese or vegetables
  • Red rice: nutty rice grown in the high valleys
  • Suja: salty butter tea

Money

The ngultrum is pegged to the Indian rupee, cash is used for most small purchases, and ATMs in towns don't always take foreign cards. Tipping guides and drivers is customary, and haggling is uncommon.

Phone data

B-Mobile and TashiCell sell SIMs with a passport, and coverage is good in towns and valleys.

Heads up

Wear long sleeves and a collar in dzongs and temples, and walk clockwise around chortens.

Bhutan travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Bhutan cost on a budget?

About $230 a day for one person, from $230 in Paro to $230 in Trashigang. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $1,610 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Bhutan?

March to May and October to November is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Bhutan?

Paro, at about $230 a day. A green valley of rice fields, the towering Rinpung Dzong, the National Museum in an old watchtower and the ancient Kyichu Lhakhang temple.

How long do you need in Bhutan?

Paro, Thimphu and Punakha. 5 to 7 days.

How much is $1 in Bhutan?

One US dollar is about 96.43 Bhutanese ngultrum (BTN), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 1,929 BTN, and a typical budget day of $230 comes to about 22,000 BTN.

How do you get around Bhutan cheaply?

Most visitors get around by hired car with a driver, arranged before arrival. Public buses link the main towns but are slow and infrequent, and there are no ride-hailing apps.

What food should you try in Bhutan?

Ema datshi, chilies cooked in cheese, the national dish. Momos, steamed dumplings with meat, cheese or vegetables. Red rice, nutty rice grown in the high valleys. Suja, salty butter tea.
