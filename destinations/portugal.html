Portugal on a budget

Western Europe's best value on a budget: tiled Lisbon and Porto, Atlantic surf beaches, cheap wine and custard tarts, and friendly hostels in every town.

Mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. Cash machines and exchange desks pay a little less.

Getting around

CP trains link Lisbon, Porto and the Algarve, and Rede Expressos and FlixBus cover longer routes cheaply. Uber and Bolt work in the cities and are often cheaper than taxis. Book long-distance trains ahead in summer, and in Lisbon and Porto load a transit card for buses and trams.

Where to sleep

Lisbon and Porto have many highly rated hostels, and guesthouses are common elsewhere. Book ahead in summer, especially in the Algarve.

What to eat

  • Pastel de nata: custard tart with flaky pastry
  • Bacalhau à Brás: shredded salt cod with eggs and fried potatoes
  • Francesinha: Porto sandwich of meats under melted cheese and beer sauce
  • Bifana: thin pork steak sandwich with garlic

Money

Cards work almost everywhere, though some small cafes prefer cash. Multibanco ATMs usually charge no local fee; tipping a few coins or rounding up is enough.

Phone data

MEO, Vodafone and NOS SIMs are easy to buy with your passport, and EU visitors can use their home SIM; coverage is good across the country.

Heads up

Bread, olives and cheese put on the table before a meal are not free, so send them back if you don't want them.

Portugal travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Portugal cost on a budget?

About $62 a day for one person, from $47 in Braga to $72 in Azores. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $434 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Portugal?

April to June and September to October is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Portugal?

Braga, at about $47 a day. Portugal's religious capital, with the baroque staircase of Bom Jesus do Monte, an ancient cathedral and lively student cafés in the old centre.

How long do you need in Portugal?

Lisbon, Sintra, Porto, then down to the Algarve. 2 weeks.

How much is $1 in Portugal?

One US dollar is about 0.889 Euro (EUR), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 17.78 EUR, and a typical budget day of $62 comes to about 55.00 EUR.

CP trains link Lisbon, Porto and the Algarve, and Rede Expressos and FlixBus cover longer routes cheaply. Uber and Bolt work in the cities and are often cheaper than taxis. Book long-distance trains ahead in summer, and in Lisbon and Porto load a transit card for buses and trams.
