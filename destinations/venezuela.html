Getting around

Canaima and the Gran Sabana are mostly reached by small domestic flights or long road journeys, usually arranged as packages through established operators. Many governments advise against travel to some states and border areas, and road travel at night is discouraged. Local apps such as Ridery and Yummy are used in Caracas instead of international ride-hailing.

Where to sleep

Lodging is mostly posadas (small guesthouses) and tour-run camps in Canaima and Roraima, and supply is limited, so it's usually booked as part of a tour.

What to eat

  • Arepa: corn cake split and stuffed with fillings
  • Pabellón criollo: shredded beef, black beans, rice and fried plantain
  • Cachapa: sweet corn pancake folded around soft cheese
  • Tequeños: fried dough sticks wrapped around white cheese

Money

US dollars in small, clean notes are widely used for everyday payments, and foreign cards often don't work. Prices are often quoted in dollars even when paid in bolívares.

Phone data

Movistar, Digitel and Movilnet SIMs need ID registration, and coverage is weak or absent in Canaima and the Gran Sabana.

Heads up

Power, water and fuel outages are frequent, and the June 2026 earthquakes damaged some infrastructure, so check conditions with any operator before going.

Venezuela travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Venezuela cost on a budget?

About $97 a day for one person, from $39 in Coro to $115 in Los Roques. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $679 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Venezuela?

December to April is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.
