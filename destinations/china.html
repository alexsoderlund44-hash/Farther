Getting around

High-speed trains cover most of the country, slower sleeper trains are cheaper, and DiDi works for rides with an English interface. Train tickets are tied to your passport, so book on the 12306 app or Trip.com ahead of holidays like Golden Week and Chinese New Year.

Where to sleep

Youth hostels are cheap and well run in most tourist cities, but some budget hotels can't register foreign guests, so check before booking.

What to eat

  • Jiaozi: boiled dumplings with meat or vegetable filling
  • Jianbing: savory crepe with egg, a street breakfast
  • Lanzhou lamian: hand-pulled noodles in beef broth
  • Mapo tofu: soft tofu in a spicy Sichuan pepper sauce

Money

Most payments go through Alipay or WeChat Pay, which both accept foreign cards, but carry some cash as backup. Tipping isn't expected, and haggling is normal at markets.

Phone data

Google, WhatsApp and many Western apps are blocked on local SIMs, so an eSIM from abroad is the simpler choice, and coverage is excellent.

China travel: quick answers

How much does a day in China cost on a budget?

About $42 a day for one person, from $34 in Xiahe to $75 in Jiuzhaigou. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $294 before flights.

When is the best time to visit China?

April to May and September to October is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in China?

Xiahe, at about $34 a day. Labrang Monastery, one of the largest Tibetan monasteries outside Tibet, sits on the edge of the rolling Sangke grasslands.

How long do you need in China?

Beijing, Xi'an, Chengdu, Yunnan and Guilin, ending in Hong Kong. 3 to 4 weeks.

How much is $1 in China?

One US dollar is about 6.71 Chinese yuan (CNY), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 134 CNY, and a typical budget day of $42 comes to about 280 CNY.

How do you get around China cheaply?

High-speed trains cover most of the country, slower sleeper trains are cheaper, and DiDi works for rides with an English interface. Train tickets are tied to your passport, so book on the 12306 app or Trip.com ahead of holidays like Golden Week and Chinese New Year.

What food should you try in China?

Jiaozi, boiled dumplings with meat or vegetable filling. Jianbing, savory crepe with egg, a street breakfast. Lanzhou lamian, hand-pulled noodles in beef broth. Mapo tofu, soft tofu in a spicy Sichuan pepper sauce.
