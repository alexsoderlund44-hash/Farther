Getting around

Buses connect the coast with the north, and the train from Bar to Podgorica and Bijelo Polje is cheap and scenic. Ride-hailing apps barely exist here, so use local taxi companies or agree on fares up front. Coastal buses fill up in July and August, so buy tickets a day ahead at the station or online.

Where to sleep

Private rooms (sobe) and apartments are the cheap choice on the coast, and Kotor and Budva have hostels. Žabljak has guesthouses and campsites, and the coast books out in high summer.

What to eat

  • Njeguški pršut: dry-cured ham from the village of Njeguši
  • Kačamak: rich cornmeal and potato porridge with cheese and kajmak
  • Black risotto: rice cooked with squid ink and seafood
  • Priganice: small fried dough balls with honey or cheese

Money

Montenegro uses the euro, and cards work in most restaurants on the coast, but carry cash for buses and small guesthouses. Rounding up or leaving about 10 percent is common.

Phone data

A local prepaid SIM from One, Crnogorski Telekom or m:tel is cheap and easy to buy, and an eSIM is handy if you are on a short visit; coverage is good on the coast and patchy in the mountains.

Heads up

Montenegro is not in the EU, so check your roaming plan before arriving, as EU roaming does not cover it.

Montenegro travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Montenegro cost on a budget?

About $51 a day for one person, from $33 in Ostrog Monastery to $75 in Tara River Canyon. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $357 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Montenegro?

May to June and September to October is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

Ostrog Monastery, at about $33 a day. Monastery carved into a sheer cliff face high above the Bjelopavlići plain, an important pilgrimage site for Orthodox Christians.
