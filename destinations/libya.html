Getting around

Travel in Libya is only practical through a specialist operator, who arranges drivers, guides and permits. Domestic flights link Tripoli with other cities, and roads between towns have checkpoints. Independent travel is not realistic at present.

Where to sleep

Hotels are arranged by your operator, and choices outside Tripoli are limited.

What to eat

  • Bazeen: barley dough dome served with a spicy lamb stew
  • Shorba libiya: tomato soup with lamb, orzo and mint
  • Couscous: steamed semolina with lamb and vegetables
  • Asida: sweet wheat porridge eaten with date syrup and butter

Money

Libya is a cash economy and foreign cards rarely work, so operators usually advise bringing enough cash in dollars or euros. Haggling is normal in markets.

Phone data

Local SIMs from Libyana and Al Madar need a passport and are best arranged through your operator, and coverage is decent in cities.

Heads up

Check your government's latest advice before any planning, and follow your operator's guidance at checkpoints.

Libya travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Libya cost on a budget?

About $65 a day for one person, from $55 in Tripoli to $65 in Cyrene. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $455 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Libya?

October to March is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Libya?

Tripoli, at about $55 a day. The old medina, the Roman Arch of Marcus Aurelius and the Red Castle museum overlooking the harbor of Libya's capital.

How long do you need in Libya?

Tripoli, Leptis Magna and Ghadames, only with a specialist operator.

How much is $1 in Libya?

One US dollar is about 6.40 Libyan dinar (LYD), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 128 LYD, and a typical budget day of $65 comes to about 420 LYD.

How do you get around Libya cheaply?

Travel in Libya is only practical through a specialist operator, who arranges drivers, guides and permits. Domestic flights link Tripoli with other cities, and roads between towns have checkpoints. Independent travel is not realistic at present.

What food should you try in Libya?

Bazeen, barley dough dome served with a spicy lamb stew. Shorba libiya, tomato soup with lamb, orzo and mint. Couscous, steamed semolina with lamb and vegetables. Asida, sweet wheat porridge eaten with date syrup and butter.

Is it safe to travel to Libya?

Most governments advise against all travel to Libya because of armed conflict, terrorism and kidnapping. Check your government's current travel advice before planning a trip.
