Know before you go

Timor-Leste on a budget: the basics

Currency

Timor-Leste uses the US dollar

No exchange needed. Bring clean, untorn notes, since small shops often refuse damaged bills.

Getting around

Microlets (minibuses) run around Dili, buses and open trucks reach the districts on rough roads, and a ferry runs to Atauro Island. There are no ride-hailing apps, so use taxis and agree fares first. Check ferry days ahead, since services are limited.

Where to sleep

Guesthouses in Dili, eco-lodges on Atauro and simple homestays in the mountains around Maubisse are the main options.

What to eat

  • Batar da'an: corn, beans and pumpkin stewed together
  • Ikan sabuko: grilled Spanish mackerel, common in Dili
  • Tukir: meat cooked with spices inside bamboo
  • Timorese coffee: locally grown arabica, strong and smooth

Money

The currency is the US dollar, ATMs are mostly in Dili, and cards are rarely accepted outside it, so carry enough cash. Tipping isn't expected, and prices are usually fixed.

Phone data

Telkomcel, Timor Telecom and Telemor sell SIMs with passport registration, with decent coverage in Dili and patchy service in the mountains.

Heads up

Malaria and dengue are present, so use repellent and cover up at dawn and dusk.

Timor-Leste travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Timor-Leste cost on a budget?

About $37 a day for one person, from $32 in Maubisse to $53 in Atauro Island. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $259 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Timor-Leste?

May to October is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Timor-Leste?

Maubisse, at about $32 a day. A cool mountain town among coffee plantations and misty hills, the gateway to Timor-Leste's highest peak.

How long do you need in Timor-Leste?

Dili, Atauro Island, then up to Maubisse and Mount Ramelau. 10 days.

How do you get around Timor-Leste cheaply?

Microlets (minibuses) run around Dili, buses and open trucks reach the districts on rough roads, and a ferry runs to Atauro Island. There are no ride-hailing apps, so use taxis and agree fares first. Check ferry days ahead, since services are limited.

What food should you try in Timor-Leste?

Batar da'an, corn, beans and pumpkin stewed together. Ikan sabuko, grilled Spanish mackerel, common in Dili. Tukir, meat cooked with spices inside bamboo. Timorese coffee, locally grown arabica, strong and smooth.
