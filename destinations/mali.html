Getting around

Within Mali, intercity buses and shared minibuses link the main towns, river pinasses (wooden boats) have long run on the Niger, and taxis and moto-taxis cover Bamako. Roads to Djenné and Dogon Country pass through areas covered by the advisory, so check current government advice before any plan.

Where to sleep

Traditional budget options are campements, simple guesthouses and mission houses, and in Dogon Country nights were often spent on village rooftops.

What to eat

  • Tô: millet or maize porridge with leaf or okra sauce
  • Riz gras: rice cooked in a rich tomato and meat sauce
  • Capitaine: grilled Nile perch from the Niger River
  • Jus de gingembre: strong, sweet ginger juice

Money

Mali is cash-based, and ATMs in Bamako generally take Visa only. Haggling is expected in markets, and tips are small.

Phone data

Orange and Moov SIMs need passport registration, and coverage is good in Bamako but limited in remote areas.

Heads up

Even in Bamako, many governments advise caution, so register with your embassy and follow local news closely.

Mali travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Mali cost on a budget?

About $36 a day for one person, from $30 in Siby to $53 in Timbuktu. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $252 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Mali?

November to February is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Mali?

Siby, at about $30 a day. Sandstone escarpment village southwest of Bamako, known for the natural Kamadjan Arch, Mandinka hunter traditions and quiet hikes across granite plateaus above the plain.

How long do you need in Mali?

Bamako, Djenné and Dogon Country. 2 weeks, if travel becomes safe.
