Getting around

Sudan is in active civil war, transport is badly disrupted, and normal travel is not possible. Before the war, buses linked Khartoum with the north and shared minibuses covered smaller routes. Any future trip should start with up-to-date official advice.

Where to sleep

Before the war, budget stays were simple hotels and basic lodges, with camping possible near Meroe. Many places have since closed or been damaged.

What to eat

  • Ful: stewed fava beans with oil and spices, the daily breakfast
  • Kisra: thin fermented sorghum flatbread, eaten with stews
  • Gurasa: thick wheat pancake served with stew
  • Karkade: hibiscus drink served cold or hot

Money

Before the war, Sudan ran almost entirely on cash, and foreign cards generally did not work. Banking remains heavily disrupted.

Phone data

Zain, MTN and Sudani were the main networks, and coverage and internet have been heavily disrupted by the conflict.

Heads up

Most governments advise against all travel to Sudan, so treat this guide as background for when conditions change.

Sudan travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Sudan cost on a budget?

About $41 a day for one person, from $38 in Jebel Barkal to $53 in Meroë Pyramids. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $287 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Sudan?

November to February is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

Jebel Barkal, at about $38 a day. A sacred flat-topped mountain rising from the desert, with temples to Amun and royal Kushite pyramids clustered at its foot.
