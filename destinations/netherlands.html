Mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. Cash machines and exchange desks pay a little less.

Getting around

NS trains link cities, and you can tap in with a contactless card on trains and most transit. Cycling is the easiest way around cities, and Uber works in Amsterdam. Off-peak train fares are cheaper.

Where to sleep

Hostels are common in Amsterdam, and campsites are cheap outside cities. Book ahead for Amsterdam in summer.

What to eat

  • Stroopwafel: thin waffles with caramel syrup
  • Bitterballen: fried breaded meat ragout balls
  • Haring: raw herring with onions
  • Poffertjes: small fluffy pancakes with powdered sugar

Money

Cards and contactless are accepted almost everywhere, but many supermarkets and cafes take only debit cards, so carry one. Tipping is optional, with rounding up common.

Phone data

KPN, Vodafone and Odido SIMs are easy to buy, and EU visitors can roam at home rates; coverage is good across the country.

Heads up

Don't walk in bike lanes, as cyclists move fast and expect them clear.

Netherlands travel: quick answers

How much does a day in Netherlands cost on a budget?

About $78 a day for one person, from $66 in Groningen to $98 in Amsterdam. That covers a hostel dorm or simple guesthouse, local food, local transport and the usual entry fees, so a week runs about $546 before flights.

When is the best time to visit Netherlands?

April to June and September is the best time, with the most reliable weather for getting around.

What is the cheapest place to visit in Netherlands?

Groningen, at about $66 a day. Student city in the north with lively bars, the striking Groninger Museum, Martini Tower and gateway to Wadden Sea mudflat walks.

How long do you need in Netherlands?

Amsterdam, Utrecht, Rotterdam and Kinderdijk. 5 days.

How much is $1 in Netherlands?

One US dollar is about 0.889 Euro (EUR), going by the mid-market rate on 2026-10-05. So $20 is roughly 17.78 EUR, and a typical budget day of $78 comes to about 69.00 EUR.

How do you get around Netherlands cheaply?

NS trains link cities, and you can tap in with a contactless card on trains and most transit. Cycling is the easiest way around cities, and Uber works in Amsterdam. Off-peak train fares are cheaper.

What food should you try in Netherlands?

Stroopwafel, thin waffles with caramel syrup. Bitterballen, fried breaded meat ragout balls. Haring, raw herring with onions. Poffertjes, small fluffy pancakes with powdered sugar.
